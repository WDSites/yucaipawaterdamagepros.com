The water problem Calimesa homes meet most

In a foothill city strung along Interstate 10, the most common call is not a river. It is a supply line, a water heater, a washing machine hose or an air conditioner condensate line that has been dripping inside a wall. These failures are quiet, and the first sign is often a soft spot in a floor or a stain spreading across a ceiling.

Ground that slopes toward a house is the other recurring cause. Calimesa runs from roughly 2,300 to 3,500 feet in elevation across the San Bernardino Mountain foothills, so lots tilt, and a hard rain can push water against a slab or into a garage. If you can see that the water came from outside, call us and read flood damage restoration for how that cleanup differs.

When water is spreading in Calimesa

Active water needs three things in quick order: stop it, get it out, and start drying. The fastest wins come from shutting off the supply and calling the emergency line before the water soaks into drywall and insulation. A crew arrives with pumps and extraction units for the standing water, then sets air movers and dehumidifiers once moisture readings show where the wet spots are.

Many Calimesa homes are on slabs, so water spreads across floor coverings instead of draining into a crawl space. That tends to make carpet, pad and baseboards the first casualties. Lifting furniture off wet carpet quickly can save pieces that would otherwise wick moisture.

If Water Is Still Spreading

Shut off the supply

Close the fixture valve under the sink or toilet, or the main shutoff for the house. If you cannot find it, check near the water meter and tell us when you call.

Cut power only when dry and safe

If water has reached outlets or the panel is in a wet garage, do not step in. Call 911 or the power utility, then call us. Otherwise switch off power to the wet rooms.

Lift and relocate

Get electronics, papers and soft furniture onto beds, counters or a dry room. Pull rugs and pad if you can do it without slipping.

Keep people and pets away

If the water came from a toilet, a sewer line or outdoor flooding, treat it as contaminated. Keep children and pets out, and wait for the crew before touching it.
