Pick the Service That Matches Your Situation

Water damage jobs move through stages, and each stage has its own tools and its own skills. A crew that is excellent at pumping out a flooded garage may not be the right crew to rebuild a ceiling, which is why this site splits the work into separate pages. Read the one that describes where you are now, and call if you are unsure.

Emergency cases usually start at the top of the list and move down. Repair and mold work tend to follow once the leak is fixed and the building has dried out.

💧

Emergency Water Removal

Water pooled on a floor, in a garage or under the house has to be pumped out immediately. Expect pumps, extraction units, and callouts for burst pipes, washer hoses and failed supply lines.

Start at emergency water removal if water is still on the floor.

🛡️

Water Mitigation

Mitigation is the first 24 to 72 hours of damage control, including moisture mapping, drying setup and structural drying behind walls and under floors.

Read about water mitigation if the visible water is gone but walls or floors still feel damp.

🧽

Water Damage Cleanup

With the source stopped, ruined drywall, saturated carpet pad and debris must leave the building, and what remains gets sanitized and aired out.

Go to water damage cleanup when that is your stage.

🌊

Flood Damage Restoration

Runoff, mud and rising groundwater bring contamination that a clean pipe does not. Garages, crawl spaces and low rooms are the usual trouble spots, and sewage backups belong on this page as well.

See flood damage restoration for the safety rules.

🔧

Water Damage Repair

Repair begins after drying is confirmed by readings. Drywall, insulation, ceilings, hardwood and paint get rebuilt so the room looks finished.

Visit water damage repair once everything is dry.

🦠

Mold Remediation

Mold needs moisture, so it often follows a leak that was missed or dried too slowly. The work follows ANSI/IICRC S520, the published mold standard.

Look at mold remediation if you can see or smell mold.

What Applies To Every Job

The Same Standards Behind Each Service

Every job starts with an assessment using moisture meters and thermal imaging, and the result is a written scope. You hear the scope and the price before any work starts, so you can compare what you are told against what is done. Our crews are licensed and insured.

Industry practice for water work is set out in ANSI/IICRC S500, and for mold in ANSI/IICRC S520. Put the same question to any restoration company you consider. Their answer shows how they handle water categories, drying goals and containment.

Permits are a local question. For structural work in Yucaipa, the city's Community Development staff can tell you what applies. Homes in Redlands or Calimesa answer to those cities instead, so call their building departments.

Cost And Insurance

What Shapes the Price of Each Service

Price follows the water category, the area affected, the materials involved, the days of equipment drying and the scope of rebuilding. Exact numbers are not given here, because a fair figure needs a look at your property. Want your real number? Call (909) 505-3870 and describe the damage.

Insurance coverage varies. A sudden and accidental loss is often covered, a slow leak or neglect often is not, and flood generally needs its own policy. Read your policy or call your insurer before deciding what to claim.

Before you call

What to Do Before You Call About Any Service

  • Close the fixture shutoff or the main valve if you can reach it without wading through water.
  • Stay out of standing water near outlets, appliances or the electrical panel.
  • Keep people and pets away from sewage or floodwater.
  • Photograph everything before cleanup begins.
  • Have the street address and your best estimate of how long the area has been wet ready for the dispatcher.

Crews work to published industry practice, and ANSI/IICRC S500 for water and S520 for mold are the documents to ask about.
